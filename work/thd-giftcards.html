01 · The Overview

Ending the detour.

The project began with a practical business need: bring gift cards in-house and let customers buy them alongside other products.

The Home Depot’s Gift Card Business team wanted to move away from a third-party provider. The existing setup limited control over design and optimization, and customers could not purchase gift cards alongside regular merchandise.

Initially scoped as an MVP for mixed-cart functionality, the work expanded to specialty gift cards and a refreshed experience informed by research. I connected the landing page, card selection, cart, and checkout so the journey could work as a whole.

02 · Research

What the reactions revealed.

Customer behavior revealed three priorities: find a card, check a balance, and understand where to go next.

Discovery was intentional.

Most visitors arrived through search or navigation links. The landing page needed to help them act on that intent quickly.

Returning visitors needed a shortcut.

A large share of repeat visits were for balance checks. That simple task deserved a clear, accessible place in the experience.

The hero sent mixed signals.

Misdirected clicks on the existing banner suggested that its visual cues did not match what customers expected to be interactive.

03 · Design

Turn the research into a clearer journey.

I translated customer needs and product requirements into the landing-page flow, then used wireframes to establish the hierarchy.

04 · Testing

Two designs. Four everyday tasks.

I ran unmoderated usability tests on desktop and mobile, asking participants to approach the experience as both purchasers and recipients.

30Participants across two designs
~5 hoursRecorded usability sessions
4 tasksFind a THD card, find a specialty card, check a balance, and find an e-gift card

Design I: useful filters and straightforward tasks.

Participants completed the tasks with ease and singled out the product-list filters as especially useful. Specialty cards were findable, but participants questioned the value of buying them from The Home Depot.

Participants were able to complete all tasks with ease (find THD card, find specialty card, check balance, shop for e-gift cards).

Participants utilized the PLP section of the page and found the filters most useful.

Participants were able to find specialty cards but did not see the value of HD.com selling them.

Design II: an opportunity to simplify.

Participants found the page cluttered and repetitive. They felt visual links belonged lower on the page, and specialty cards did not align with their expectations of the retailer.

Specialty cards were findable but didn't align with customer expectations for HD.com.

Participants felt the visual links needed to be further down the page, or not as high on the page.

Participants felt that the page was cluttered and repetitive, and could be simplified to make the experience more streamlined.

The direction combined Design I’s straightforward task flow with the warmer, doer-focused tone explored in Design II, while removing clutter.

05 · Checkout

Keep gift cards in the shopping journey.

Moving away from third-party checkout meant designing how physical and digital gift cards worked within The Home Depot’s own cart and checkout.

06 · The Outcome

A connected experience, with room to grow.

The work brought mixed-cart functionality, integrated checkout, and clearer gift card discovery into one experience.

Research shaped both the practical tasks and the visual direction. Customers could move from finding a card to purchasing it within The Home Depot’s shopping experience, while the business gained more control over the journey and future improvements.

The central lesson: a gifting experience must serve the quick, transactional visit as carefully as the more exploratory purchase.
