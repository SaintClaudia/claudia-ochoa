The Home Depot

Simplifying gifting, from discovery to checkout.

An end-to-end gift card experience that brings discovery, choice, and checkout into one customer journey.

5 min read

Role
Interaction Design · Research · UX/UI
Scope
Cart · Checkout · Discovery · Gift Cards
Research
30 usability participants
Platform
Desktop & Mobile Web
01 · The Overview

Ending the detour.

The project began with a practical business need: bring gift cards in-house and let customers buy them alongside other products.

The Home Depot’s Gift Card business team wanted to move away from a third-party provider. The existing setup limited control over design and optimization, and customers could not purchase gift cards alongside regular merchandise.

Initially scoped as an MVP for mixed-cart functionality, the work expanded to specialty gift cards and a refreshed experience informed by research. I connected the landing page, card selection, cart, and checkout so the journey could work as a whole.

02 · Research

Customer Insights

Customer behavior revealed two clear priorities: choosing a gift card and checking an existing balance.

  • 1Discovery began with intent. Most visitors arrived through search or navigation links. The landing page needed to confirm they were in the right place and make gift card designs easy to explore.
  • 2Returning visitors needed a shortcut. Many repeat visits centered on checking a balance. This frequent task needed a clear, accessible place in the experience.
  • 3The hero sent mixed signals. Misdirected clicks on the existing banner suggested that its visual cues did not match customers' expectations.

What the signals were conveying.

The top navigational bar showed unusually heavy activity — a sign that people weren't browsing, they were searching, because they couldn't find what they were looking for. Usability testing later confirmed why: several participants said the hero image made them believe they were on the garden page rather than the gift card page.

The "Check Gift Card Balance" tile also drew significantly more engagement than the others. That told me that checking their gift card balance wasn't a secondary utility — it was one of the primary reasons people were arriving on this page at all. The direction became clear: make the page unmistakably about gift cards and give check balance the prominence customers deserved.

Competitive landscape.

Looking at how other retailers stack up, a few patterns stood out. Amazon and Walmart lead on design and form flexibility, while Target and Starbucks stand out for occasion-based shopping and specialty cards. Almost everyone has mixed-cart enablement fully built out.

That gave me a clear picture of where Home Depot could position itself: rather than trying to compete everywhere, focus on the things that support the customer journey first.

03 · Design

Turn the research into a clear path.

I translated customer needs and product requirements into the landing-page flow, then used wireframes to establish the hierarchy.

Design, product, and business landed on testing two options.

Design I tested how a new, gift-card-focused hero would perform against the existing one — introducing specialty gift cards before leading into a Home Depot-specific product landing page.

Design II kept the hero and secondary links identical to the existing page, adding a category for best-selling Home Depot gift cards along with category links to both the Home Depot and specialty landing pages, followed by a mixed assortment for the PLP section.

04 · Testing

Two designs. Four everyday tasks.

I ran unmoderated usability tests on desktop and mobile, asking participants to approach the experience as both purchasers and recipients.

Design I: Grounded in initial research.

Participants completed the tasks with ease and singled out the product-list filters as especially useful. Specialty cards were findable, but participants questioned the value of buying them from The Home Depot.

Design II: Grounded in competitive research.

Participants found the page cluttered and repetitive. They felt visual links belonged lower on the page, and specialty cards did not align with their expectations of the retailer.

05 · Refinement

Combine the best of both directions.

Testing didn't crown a single winner — it revealed which parts of each design to keep, fix, or drop.

The final design paired Design I's gift-card-centered hero with feedback from Design II, which suggested pushing secondary links further down the page. Both tests also surfaced strong customer sentiment toward specialty gift cards, so that was addressed directly in the new design.

Check balance moved into the hero, specialty cards stayed reachable through pagination rather than crowding the primary assortment, and the redundant promotional sections from Design II were moved so the product listing could load above the fold. The result was tested, approved, and later launched.

06 · The Outcome

A connected experience, with room to grow.

The work brought mixed-cart functionality, integrated checkout, and clearer gift card discovery into one experience.

Research shaped both the practical tasks and the visual direction. Customers could move from finding a card to purchasing it within The Home Depot’s shopping experience, while the business gained more control over the journey and future improvements.

The central lesson: a gifting experience must serve the quick, transactional visit as carefully as the more exploratory purchase.

The Home Depot's mission statement is to provide the highest level of service, the broadest selection of products, and the most competitive prices (The Home Depot, Inc., n.d.). This redesign put that mission into practice for gift cards specifically:

  • 1Service. Made check balance and checkout effortless.
  • 2Selection. Gave gift cards a clear home alongside the core product.
  • 3Value. Kept gift cards inside the same experience customers already trust.
