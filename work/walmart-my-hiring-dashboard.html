01 · Overview

Overview

I led the design of My Hiring Dashboard — a 0→1 experience within Me@Campus built to help corporate hiring managers, across both in-store and Home Office roles, track, manage, and complete hiring tasks more efficiently.

There were no established internal frameworks, tokens, or reusable patterns in place for this product area, requiring many foundational design decisions to be made from scratch. The goal was to reduce friction, close process gaps, and build trust for hiring managers navigating high-volume corporate hiring by surfacing the right information at the right time.

02 · The Problem

Problem Statement

Corporate hiring managers were required to navigate multiple disconnected systems and communication channels to manage job postings, interviews, candidate progress, and onboarding steps. There was no single, centralized view of hiring status, making it difficult to identify bottlenecks, take timely action, or maintain accountability across teams.

This fragmentation resulted in duplicated effort and inconsistent visibility into where things stood across the hiring process, while adding to the cognitive load of hiring managers who were already balancing this responsibility alongside their day-to-day work.

03 · Research & Strategy

Research and Strategy

To understand the problem space, I partnered closely with recruiters and corporate hiring managers to map their end-to-end workflows. This included identifying system handoffs and moments where managers lacked clarity on what action to take next.

  • 1Interviewed corporate hiring managers and recruiters to understand workflows, pain points, and information needs
  • 2Identified gaps between recruiting, HR partners, and hiring managers
  • 3Synthesized findings into core user needs: visibility, accountability, and clear next steps

Visibility

See where things stand

A single place to check the status of every open role and candidate, instead of piecing it together across systems.

Accountability

Know who owns what

Clear ownership when something is stalled, instead of a gap that's easy for any one team to assume someone else is covering.

Clear next steps

Know what to do next

Not just a status, but the next action — schedule, follow up, give feedback — surfaced without having to go looking for it.

04 · Design Process

UX and Interaction Design

I designed My Hiring Dashboard as a 0→1 initiative within Me@Campus, aligning with Walmart's broader internal tools that support communication, productivity, and people operations.

  • 1Created flows, wireframes, and high-fidelity prototypes that surfaced hiring status, alerts, and quick actions
  • 2Ran usability testing with managers to validate clarity, task completion, and cognitive load
  • 3Iterated based on feedback from stakeholders across People Product & Design to align with evolving roadmap priorities
05 · My Hiring Dashboard

See what needs attention today.

The dashboard brings new applicants, scheduled interviews, and pending feedback into one place, helping hiring managers identify their next action.

Dashboard, Requisitions, and Candidates provide distinct views of the hiring workflow. The dashboard prioritizes the day’s work: review new applicants, view interview details, and follow up on outstanding feedback.

06 · Create Requisition

Shape the role before moving forward.

Creating a requisition is a separate experience, focused on defining the role and preparing its summary.

The role title and key details remain visible above an editable role summary. A progress indicator establishes where the manager is in the creation flow, while the mobile keyboard supports writing and refining the description.

07 · Outcome

Results and Impact

My Hiring Dashboard was prioritized as a foundational component of Walmart’s broader hiring and recruiting modernization roadmap. The intended benefit was to help managers spend less time navigating administrative tasks and more time building their teams.

08 · Takeaways

Key Takeaways

Designing a single point of entry for hiring tasks reinforced the importance of visibility and momentum in complex internal workflows. This work also highlighted the value of stepping back to understand ecosystem-wide dependencies before anchoring on individual surfaces — an insight that informed how later phases of the project were approached.
