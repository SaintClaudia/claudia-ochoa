01 · Overview

Built from scratch, with no existing patterns to lean on.

I led the design of My Hiring Dashboard — a 0→1 experience within Me@Campus built to help corporate hiring managers, across both in-store and Home Office roles, track, manage, and complete hiring tasks more efficiently.

There were no established internal frameworks, tokens, or reusable patterns in place for this product area, requiring many foundational design decisions to be made from scratch. The goal was to reduce friction, close process gaps, and build trust for hiring managers navigating high-volume corporate hiring by surfacing the right information at the right time.

02 · The Problem

Piecing it together, every time.

Corporate hiring managers were required to navigate multiple disconnected systems and communication channels to manage job postings, interviews, candidate progress, and onboarding steps.

There was no single, centralized view of hiring status, making it difficult to identify bottlenecks, take timely action, or maintain accountability across teams. This fragmentation resulted in duplicated effort and inconsistent visibility into where things stood across the hiring process, while adding to the cognitive load of hiring managers who were already balancing this responsibility alongside their day-to-day work.

03 · Research & Strategy

Talking to the people doing the work.

To understand the problem space, I partnered closely with recruiters and corporate hiring managers to map their end-to-end workflows.

This included identifying system handoffs and moments where managers lacked clarity on what action to take next.

  • 1Interviewed corporate hiring managers and recruiters to understand workflows, pain points, and information needs
  • 2Identified gaps between recruiting, HR partners, and hiring managers
  • 3Synthesized findings into core user needs: visibility, accountability, and clear next steps
04 · Design Process

Systems thinking.

I designed My Hiring Dashboard as a 0→1 initiative within Me@Campus, aligning with Walmart's broader internal tools that support communication, productivity, and people operations.

  • 1Created flows, wireframes, and high-fidelity prototypes that surfaced hiring status, alerts, and quick actions
  • 2Ran usability testing with managers to validate clarity, task completion, and cognitive load
  • 3Iterated based on stakeholder feedback to align with evolving roadmap priorities
05 · My Hiring Dashboard

The day, at a glance.

The dashboard surfaces new applicants, upcoming interviews, and pending feedback in one place — so hiring managers always know their next action. Requisitions and Candidates offer deeper views into the workflow when more detail is needed.

My Hiring dashboard
06 · Create Requisition

Shape the role.

Designing this flow meant first understanding the process itself — questioning who was responsible for each step and why. That research surfaced a structural gap: hiring managers were creating and shaping requisitions before recruiters were ever involved, adding steps and delay before approval. The redesigned flow shifted that ownership — having the recruiter initiate the requisition — so hiring managers could focus on shaping the role rather than owning the process end-to-end.

The role title and key details remain visible above an editable role summary. A progress indicator establishes where the manager is in the creation flow, while the mobile keyboard supports writing and refining the description.

07 · The Outcome

Designed with the hiring manager in mind.

My Hiring Dashboard was prioritized as a foundational component of Walmart’s broader hiring and recruiting modernization roadmap.

The intended benefit was to help managers spend less time navigating administrative tasks and more time building their teams. Designing a single point of entry for hiring tasks reinforced the importance of visibility and momentum in complex internal workflows. This work also highlighted the value of stepping back to understand ecosystem-wide dependencies before anchoring on individual surfaces.

Walmart's People Product & Design organization exists to build world-class people experiences that attract, enable, and grow the workforce of tomorrow. My Hiring Dashboard was built for the "enable" and "grow" parts of that mission: giving hiring managers one clear, connected place to move candidates forward, so they could spend less time managing systems and more time building the teams that keep the business staffed.
