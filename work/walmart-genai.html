02 · The Problem

Millions of candidates. Too many systems.

Hourly applicants moved among disconnected tools, repeated information, completed a long assessment, and received little sense of progress. The technology reflected the organization chart rather than the candidate's goal.

I mapped the journey from job search through interview scheduling and offer acceptance. I also completed a competitor's chat-to-apply flow through the in-store interview, which exposed an operational issue beyond the screen: the handoff had to work for store associates as well as candidates.

The leadership question was not “Where can we add GenAI?” It was “Where can one intelligent, conversational layer remove fragmentation without hiding important decisions from the candidate?”

03 · The Direction

Start with the human job to be done.

The concept used AI as an orchestration and communication layer—not as decoration, and not as an opaque decision-maker.

01

One conversation

Carry context across application, scheduling, offer, and onboarding so candidates do not have to understand the systems behind the experience.

02

Progress before commitment

Let candidates explore roles and availability before requiring an account. Ask for information only when its value is clear.

03

Human tone, clear status

Use plain language, explain what is happening, and make the next step visible rather than asking candidates to trust a black box.

04

Designed handoffs

Connect digital and in-store moments so the experience works for candidates, recruiters, hiring managers, and store teams.

04 · The Experience

Make the path feel like progress.

The prototype demonstrated how the AI guide could reduce cognitive load while keeping candidate choice visible.

05 · Responsible Scale

A compelling concept still needs an operating model.

Experience direction is the beginning. Production AI leadership must define how the system earns trust, how it is evaluated, and when a person takes over.

Leadership decisionWhat it protectsHow I would validate it
Bound the AI's roleAI guides, explains, and orchestrates; employment decisions remain governed, reviewable, and appropriate to policy.Document allowed actions, prohibited actions, escalation points, and accountable owners.
Ground every answerRole, location, availability, policy, and status information must come from approved systems rather than model inference.Test source attribution, freshness, conflict handling, and behavior when data is missing.
Design human recoveryCandidates need a clear route to support when the AI is uncertain, wrong, or inappropriate for the situation.Measure escalation success, repeat contacts, abandonment, and time to resolution.
Evaluate outcomes and equityEfficiency cannot come at the expense of comprehension, accessibility, or fair treatment.Track completion, accuracy, candidate confidence, accessibility, segmented failure rates, and adverse-impact signals.
Roll out deliberatelyRecruiters, hiring managers, store teams, legal, HR, security, and candidates all experience the change differently.Pilot bounded journeys, review failures, train teams, publish ownership, and expand only when evidence supports it.
What leadership adds AI strategy is not a feature list. It is the discipline of connecting a valuable use case to accountable decisions, reliable evidence, human recovery, and organizational adoption.
06 · The Outcome

A concept that became a platform direction.

The 24-hour concept earned leadership buy-in and repositioned the work from exploration toward a new candidate application experience.

I was positioned as Creative Director for the initiative and held the experience vision through delivery, while a delivery partner integrated the direction into the broader hiring platform.

The enduring lesson: speed created the opening, but clarity created alignment. The work succeeded because it gave leaders, delivery teams, and candidates one understandable picture of what AI was there to do.

AI should make the experience more human—not less.
