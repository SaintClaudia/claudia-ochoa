## Decision

For **1st-and-10 at SF35, tied in Q1**, Lead Left from 21 personnel remains the conditional analyst first choice when the actual lead/line matchup accounts for the front. Pocket dropback with underneath releases is close. Protected play-action with a tight-end cross is third. The evidence does not establish a clear effectiveness margin between the first two.

Category: **Emerging opportunity**. None of the exact proposed calls qualifies as a validated advantage. A protected play-action combination from the same 21-personnel look remains an **experimental concept**, not a claim that SF has never used it.

The scenario assumes normal home-game conditions. Personnel uses public information available November 3; evaluate the required roles and relevant replacement evidence without penalizing backup status alone. Private playbook assignments are not a prerequisite for concept selection. The staff supplies operational call, protection, progression and check language.

## What changes the ranking

- Interior penetration, a compressed entry or an extra unaccounted fitter removes the run preference.
- Favor the pocket-pass alternative when its protection and route answer fit the unfavorable run look.
- Observed downhill commitment can promote protected play-action if protection and coverage support the intended route.
- A crowded line can become a four-man rush with linebackers dropping. Do not automatically force a hot throw.
- Unsupported replacement execution of an essential role requires reranking. A roster move or injury alone does not prove an advantage.
